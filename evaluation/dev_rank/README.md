# Development-only bilingual ranking experiment

This is a development diagnostic, not a held-out or representative accuracy estimate. The 24 inputs and proposed outputs in `corpus.json` were authored during implementation, have not been reviewed by Nepali speakers, and were inspected to decide on the rule. Literal and English examples are included but do not exhaust the possible inputs. The earlier `phase2/mixed-v1.json` had already been inspected and is a regression set only. No new holdout was opened during this experiment.

## Candidate change

A dictionary-known English word with no authored Nepali override and one mined Nepali reading can be converted by strong Nepali neighbor scores even when adjacent to clear English text. The candidate rule preserves its original and marks it uncertain only when at least one adjacent token (with no intervening punctuation) is an explicit common English word without an authored Nepali entry or is a dictionary word with neither an authored nor a mined Nepali candidate. The original and Nepali alternatives remain selectable. Authored Nepali overrides and the previous broad English/Nepali context ranking otherwise remain unchanged. **The candidate was rejected and reverted**: the author-separated frozen probe had no changed outputs, so it did not establish benefit, while the structural sample showed large possible harm for Nepali homographs. The after artifacts describe the rejected experiment, not the current engine. Dictionary membership includes names, and missing Nepali indexes do not prove English; this can leave intended Nepali homographs in Roman. It is not part of the current engine.

- Development corpus: 12/24 exact sentences before, 13/24 after. Only `ready` changed; other errors remain in spelling, ambiguity and joined-particle conventions. Exact-reference spelling and spacing may have alternatives.
- Previously inspected 20-sentence mixed regression: 34/34 English tokens, 40/43 Nepali tokens and 17/20 exact sentences after; unchanged from the recorded baseline. The corpus is not a held-out estimate.
- A deliberately artificial structural stress sample selected 500 dictionary keys also carrying exactly one mined reading and no authored override. In `mero X cha`, neither version preserved any of these 500 under the synthetic expected template. In `mero ticket X cha`, the old version preserved 0/500 and the candidate 500/500. These are not labeled real-world English tokens: some entries can be names or intended Nepali; the test measures the scope of behavior change only. The full eligible set contained 15,123 keys, and real exposure/accuracy is unknown.
- Under the experimental rule, 29 Swift core tests passed, including known English overlap, retained candidate, authored Nepali override and punctuation-boundary regressions. The experiment and its added assertion were removed; the current 28-test suite was rerun.
- Twelve process-level runs of the 24-sentence fixture with local indexes gave median 25.98 ms before and 25.98 ms after (max 28.43 vs 26.38 ms). These tiny sequential samples include launch/JSON overhead; they are not keystroke or device benchmarks and do not establish any speed difference.

`Transliterator.before.swift` is a saved pre-change source for reproducibility; `main.swift` is the standalone evaluation runner. `before.json`, `restricted-dev.json`, `mixed-restricted.json`, the stress samples/results and `timing.json` record the evidence. A broader original proposal preserved every dictionary/mined overlap, regardless of context; it affected 500/500 even with only Nepali neighbors and was narrowed before freezing. `after.json` and `overlap-after.json` are from that abandoned broad proposal, not current candidate behavior. `anchor-dev.json` records another abandoned neighbor-anchor experiment that introduced an unrelated regression.

Reproduce on this Mac: compile either `Transliterator.before.swift` or the project `Sources/NepaliCore/Transliterator.swift`, with `Composer.swift`, `GeneratedIndexes.swift`, `GeneratedAliasIndex.swift` and this directory's `main.swift` (copied as `main.swift`) using Swift. Set `NEPTYPE_ALIAS_INDEX` and `NEPTYPE_AKSHARANTAR_INDEX` to the local compiled indexes and pass `corpus.json` or either stress sample. The harness compares exact strings; no real keyboard, host, secure field or typing latency is tested. Source licensing/provenance remains in the parent project.

## Suggestion-only `ch` / `chh` experiment

After the auto-ranking experiment was rejected and reverted, a separate candidate-only change was explored. It queries at most three single-occurrence `ch` ↔ `chh` spelling variants from the existing Aksharantar index, adds at most two distinct alternatives, and only does so for lowercase ASCII 3–32 character inputs that are not in the English dictionary and whose exact mined entry has fewer than two results. It keeps exact/base candidates ahead of variants, preserves the Roman option and never uses a variant as the automatic selection. It is a heuristic for a common Roman spelling convention, not a phonetic model or a claim of frequency; dictionary names, dialects and consonant aspiration mean some new suggestions can be wrong. Mobile platforms with separate Java lookup do not gain this behavior automatically.

On `variant-dev.json`, an eight-entry deliberately selected, unreviewed development set for this convention, the expected spelling appeared among four visible alternatives in 5/8 before and 8/8 after. This is targeted development evidence and not a held-out accuracy estimate. Top choices in those eight and rendered output for the 24-sentence development corpus were unchanged. The previously inspected 20-sentence mixed regression stayed at 34/34 English tokens, 40/43 Nepali tokens and 17/20 exact sentences. Twenty-nine Swift tests passed. Warm `candidates(for:)` timing in one Mac process over 1,200 repeated mixed lookups gave median/p95 0.0526/0.0768 ms before and 0.0591/0.0818 ms after; this is a small synthetic hot-cache test, not a host, cold-load or device benchmark. `variant-runner.swift` and `benchmark.swift`, along with the JSON results, are included for reproducibility. The parent evaluated this candidate against a separately authored frozen probe and reported no recall gain and added competing options in two cases. On that evidence the expansion and its new assertion were rejected and reverted: the after artifacts above describe an abandoned candidate, not the current engine. The current 28-test Swift suite was rerun after reversion.
